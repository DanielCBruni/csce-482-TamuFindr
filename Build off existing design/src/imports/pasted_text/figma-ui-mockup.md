Create a polished, high-fidelity Figma UI mockup for a centralized Texas A&M University lost and found platform called “findr.” Today, multiple campus buildings manage lost and found separately. This platform should let students, faculty, and staff search across campus through one interface while allowing each building to manage the items physically held there. Make the distinction clear: one shared platform, multiple pickup locations. Audience and goals - Students, faculty, and staff need to find missing belongings, report lost or found items, and track claims. - Building staff need to log items, review claims, and coordinate pickup. - The experience should feel trustworthy, welcoming, accessible, and easy to use on a phone. Visual direction Use Texas A&M’s recognizable maroon and white palette, supported by warm neutrals, charcoal text, and restrained status colors. Aim for a modern university service with clear typography, generous spacing, subtle borders, and strong visual hierarchy. Avoid an overly corporate dashboard aesthetic. Use a text-based university identity unless approved brand assets are available; do not invent an official seal or logo. Create these connected screens: 1. Browse and search — desktop - University identity, platform name, navigation, and university sign-in. - Headline: “Lost something on campus?” - Prominent search input: “Search for a water bottle, keys, backpack…” - Primary actions: “Report a lost item” and “Report a found item.” - Filters for category, building or area, date found, and color. - Found-item cards with photo, general description, date found, and pickup location. - Clearly distinguish where an item was found from where it is being held. - Include realistic sample locations such as Memorial Student Center, Evans Library, and Zachry Engineering Education Complex. Treat all listings as fictional examples. 2. Found-item detail and claim - Large item photo, general description, date found, found location, and holding location. - Prominent “This might be mine” action. - Explain that ownership must be verified before pickup. - Claim form asks for identifying details that are not publicly displayed. - Keep sensitive identifiers, item contents, and unique ownership details private. - After submission, show “Claim under review” and a clear explanation of the next step. Reveal pickup instructions after approval. 3. Report a lost or found item - Begin with a simple choice: “I lost something” or “I found something.” - Use a short, guided form with category, description, approximate date, campus location, and optional photo. - Separate public description from private identifying details. - For found items, ask whether the finder still has the item or has handed it to a campus location. - Do not label an item “Ready for pickup” until staff confirms receipt. - Finish with a confirmation screen and a clear next action. 4. My activity - Sections for lost reports, found reports, and claims. - Use clear statuses such as “Open,” “Possible match,” “Claim under review,” “Approved for pickup,” and “Returned.” - Show a possible-match notification with “Review item” as the action. Make clear that a suggested match does not confirm ownership. 5. Building staff workspace — desktop - Building-scoped inventory with an option for authorized administrators to view all locations. - Searchable table with item thumbnail, item ID, category, date received, holding location, status, and next action. - Quick action to “Log a found item.” - Claim-review panel with private ownership details, approve or request-more-information actions, and pickup coordination. - Workflow for marking an item returned. - Small summary cards for items in storage, pending claims, and completed returns. - Public visitors must not see staff notes, claimant contact details, or private identifying information. 6. Mobile layouts Create mobile versions of browse/search, item detail, and report submission. Optimize for one-handed use with large touch targets, a filter sheet, and prominent primary actions. Interaction and content requirements - Prototype the flow: search → item detail → submit claim → claim under review → approved pickup instructions. - Also connect report submission → confirmation → My activity. - Include loading, no-results, empty-activity, and form-validation states. - Use realistic microcopy and fictional item data instead of lorem ipsum. - Do not invent university policies, retention periods, SSO integrations, or verified office hours. Use clearly labeled placeholders where those details are needed. - Design for accessible contrast, visible focus states, labeled inputs, and statuses that do not rely on color alone. Figma construction Use auto layout, reusable components, consistent spacing, and shared color and typography styles or variables. Include variants for buttons, inputs, item cards, filter chips, and status badges. Organize pages into Foundations, Student Experience, Staff Workspace, and Prototype. Name frames and layers clearly. Prioritize a complete, coherent user journey and a strong browse/search screen. Make reasonable design decisions and proceed without asking about minor preferences.
